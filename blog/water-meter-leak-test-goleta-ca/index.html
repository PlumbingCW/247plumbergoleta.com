How to Do a Water Meter Leak Test at Home in Goleta, CA

To run a water meter leak test, turn off every tap and appliance that uses water, read the meter, wait at least half an hour without using any water and read it again. If the numbers moved, or the small leak indicator on the dial is turning, water is escaping somewhere. That is the core of how to check for a hidden leak in Goleta, CA.

The test costs nothing and takes a flashlight and a screwdriver. It tells you whether a leak exists, and a few quick follow-ups help narrow down where it is.

The meter test, start to finish

Do this when nobody is home or the house is quiet, so no one flushes or runs a tap halfway through.

  1. 1Turn off everything that uses water. Shut off taps, showers, the dishwasher, washing machine and the ice maker. Stop any irrigation timers and make sure nothing is filling a pool or a fountain.
  2. 2Locate the meter. It usually sits in a ground-level box near the curb or the property line, with a metal or plastic lid. Lift the lid with a screwdriver and look inside for spiders before reaching in.
  3. 3Read the dial and the leak indicator. Note the full number on the register. Then look at the small triangle, star or low-flow wheel. It spins whenever any water flows, even a tiny trickle.
  4. 4Wait without using water. EPA WaterSense suggests a two-hour gap with no water use for the most reliable result. Half an hour will show a large leak, while a slow one needs the longer wait.
  5. 5Read it again. If the numbers advanced or the indicator kept moving, water is leaking somewhere on the property side of the meter.

Reading the dial and knowing the units

Meters count water in either gallons or cubic feet, and the unit is printed on the face. One cubic foot is about 7.5 gallons, so a small change in cubic feet is more water than it looks. Write down every digit, including the sweep hand or small test dial if there is one.

Most residential meters have a low-flow indicator that is far more sensitive than the main numbers. If it spins with every fixture off, you do not need to wait for the register to change. Take a photo of the dial before and after so you have a record for the plumber or your water provider.

Fixes for a found leak are made to the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the utility side of the meter is not yours to work on.

If the meter moved, narrow down where

Once the test says there is a leak, a few checks sort the usual suspects before anyone digs or opens a wall.

Toilets

Silent flapper leaks are the most common source. See the dye test below.

Outdoor spigots and irrigation

Look for wet soil, a soggy lawn, or a valve box full of water. A broken sprinkler head can run unseen.

The main line to the house

Warm or damp spots on a slab, or a patch of lawn that stays green, can mean a pressurized line is leaking underground.

Water heater and fixtures

Check the base of the heater, under sinks and behind the washing machine for drips or damp cabinet floors.

For a leak you cannot find, leak detection uses acoustic and thermal tools to find the source without tearing up finished surfaces.

The toilet dye test

Toilets leak into the bowl without making a sound, and they waste more water than most people expect. This takes about ten minutes.

  1. 1Take the lid off the tank. Do not flush during the test.
  2. 2Add food coloring. A few drops of dark food coloring in the tank water is enough.
  3. 3Wait about ten minutes. EPA WaterSense uses this window for the test.
  4. 4Check the bowl. If color shows up in the bowl without a flush, the flapper or its seat is leaking. Flush right away to avoid staining the tank.

A worn flapper is a cheap fix, and EPA WaterSense says flappers should be replaced at least every five years.

What a rising water bill means

A bill that jumps with no change in habits is a signal to test the meter. EPA WaterSense says the average household wastes more than 9,300 gallons a year to leaks, and that a drip every second adds up to over 3,000 gallons a year. A hidden leak under a slab wastes far more, and a toilet that runs wastes water without any sound.

If the test shows a leak and the plumbing is in the slab, a slab leak repair may be the answer. Do not wait. Goleta Water District's rule is that leaks must be fixed in 24 hours, and it stayed in effect after the 2019 shortage ended.

Fix it yourself or call a plumber

Many leaks are a simple part swap. Some are not, and the difference is the location.

Fine to fix yourself

  • A worn toilet flapper or fill valve
  • A dripping hose bib washer
  • A loose compression nut under a sink
  • A broken sprinkler head

Call a plumber

  • A meter that spins with every fixture checked
  • Damp floor, warm slab or hissing in a wall
  • A visible leak at the main line or the water heater
  • Any leak you cannot find after an hour of looking

When water is already pouring, turn off the main valve at the house first, then call.
