Cleaning Drains With Baking Soda and Vinegar: What Works and What Does Not

Baking soda and vinegar will freshen a drain and loosen a thin film of soap and grease. They will not dissolve hair, break up a solid clog or clear a main line. If water is standing in the sink, the mix is the wrong tool.

Used on a slow but moving drain, it is a cheap, low risk first step. The rest of this post covers how to do it, why Goleta, CA hard water changes the result and when to stop and call.

What the fizz actually does

Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, a mild base. Vinegar is a weak acid, mostly acetic acid in water. When they meet, they react and release carbon dioxide, which is the foam you see. The bubbles can nudge loose a thin layer of residue near the drain opening.

The reaction also uses up most of both ingredients. What is left is mostly water and a salt called sodium acetate, which has very little cleaning power. That is why the dramatic foam rarely reaches a clog farther down the pipe.

Pouring baking soda first and vinegar second keeps the bubbling in the drain instead of in the sink. A cup of baking soda and a cup of vinegar is the usual amount for a kitchen or bathroom sink.

The traps and vents that make a drain work are set by the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so a clog that keeps returning may be a layout problem rather than a cleaning problem.

What it can do and what it cannot

A fair test of any home remedy is whether it removes the cause. Here is how this one stacks up.

It can help with

  • Mild odor from a film in the drain opening
  • Soap residue near the strainer
  • A slow drain that still moves water
  • Routine monthly freshening of a bathroom sink

It cannot fix

  • A hair clog that has formed a mat in the trap
  • A grease plug further down the line
  • Tree roots in a sewer lateral
  • A toilet or main line that backs up

If the water is standing, adding more liquid just raises the level. Use a plunger or call, and do not pour in anything else.

How to use baking soda and vinegar safely

Keep it simple and keep it to drains that still move water.

  1. 1Clear standing water first. Bail the sink with a cup so the mix reaches the pipe instead of floating on top.
  2. 2Pour in about a cup of baking soda. Use a funnel if the drain is narrow, and tap the powder down with a spoon.
  3. 3Add a cup of warm vinegar. Cover the drain with a plug or a wet cloth so the foam works downward.
  4. 4Wait 15 to 30 minutes. Longer waits do not add much, since the reaction ends quickly.
  5. 5Flush with hot tap water. Run the tap for a minute or two. Use hot tap water rather than boiling, because boiling water can soften plastic pipe joints.

Do not mix vinegar with bleach or with a commercial drain cleaner. The combination can release harmful fumes.

Hard water changes the story

Vinegar dissolves some calcium scale, which is the white crust hard water leaves on fixtures. That makes it useful on a showerhead or aerator you can soak. It does very little to scale inside a buried drain pipe, because the contact time is short and the mix is spent after a few minutes.

Goleta water and sewer notes for drain care

Goleta Water District's 2025 water quality report gives a customer average of 21 grains per gallon, and the district calls the supply very hard. Hard water makes soap form a sticky curd instead of rinsing away, so bathroom drains build a film faster than in soft water areas. A monthly vinegar rinse on strainers and aerators helps, but it will not clean the pipe wall.

Goleta Sanitary District's October 2021 newsletter says fats, oils and grease belong in the trash, not the drain, and that wipes should not be flushed. Home remedies cannot remove grease that has already hardened inside the line, so keeping it out is the real fix.

Why people reach for this remedy and what to do instead

The mix is cheap, safe for most pipes and easy to find in any kitchen. That makes it a sensible first move on a slow drain. The mistake is treating it as a cure for a clog that has been building for months.

For hair in a bathroom sink, a plastic drain strip or a hand-held snake pulls it out directly. For a kitchen drain with grease, a cup of dish soap followed by hot tap water breaks some of the fat. A plunger helps where there is a seal over the drain.

A plumber's cable or jetter reaches past the trap, scrapes the pipe wall and rinses the debris away. That is the difference between a drain that clears for a week and one that stays clear.

Rentals and shared drains

In Isla Vista, where Census figures show only 1.2 percent of homes are owner-occupied, a pour-in remedy in one unit does nothing if the blockage sits in a line that serves several units. Tell the landlord or property manager, who can call the 24/7 line.

In Montecito, which is almost entirely single-family estates, a recurring slow drain usually points to that house's own pipes. A camera look finds out whether the cause is grease, scale or a root.
