How to Shut Off the Water to Your House in Goleta, CA

To shut off the water to your house, find the main shutoff valve where the supply pipe enters the building and close it. A round wheel handle turns clockwise until it stops, and a lever handle turns a quarter turn so it sits crosswise to the pipe. Know the spot before you need it, because a burst pipe in Goleta, CA can release gallons a minute.

Do not operate the valve at the water meter. That one belongs to the utility, and the house valve is the one you are meant to use.

Two kinds of main valve

Most homes have one of two valve types. Knowing which you have tells you how to close it and how likely it is to fail.

A gate valve can snap if forced. Turn it by hand and stop when it feels firm.

Where the main valve usually is

The shutoff is on the pipe where water first enters the house. In many homes that is on an outside wall near the front or side, in the garage, in a utility closet or near the water heater. In homes with a basement or crawl space it is often where the pipe comes through the foundation.

Follow the supply pipe from the front of the property toward the house. Look for a pressure regulator, a bell-shaped device on the line, since the main valve is usually just before or after it. If you still cannot find it, check around the water heater and along walls closest to the street.

The house-side valve is part of the plumbing the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects to be accessible, so a valve that is buried or seized is worth replacing.

If water is pouring right now

A burst pipe or failed supply line calls for speed and order. Do these in sequence.

Close the main valve

Turn it all the way off. If the water slows to a dribble, the supply is shut.

Cut the power if water is near outlets

Switch off the breaker for any wet room if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

Open a low faucet

Open a tap on the lowest floor and flush a toilet to drain the pipes and relieve pressure.

Call us

Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher what burst, where the water is and whether it has stopped.

Move rugs, electronics and stored items out of the wet area while you wait. If the leak is behind a wall or under a slab, burst pipe repair starts with locating the break.

The valve at the meter is not yours to turn

The meter sits in a ground-level box near the curb or property line. Next to it, on the utility's side of the meter, is a shutoff that belongs to the water provider. Customers should not operate it. Turning it can damage the stem, and a damaged utility valve can leave the whole street without a shutoff.

Some meter boxes also have a second valve on the house side of the meter. Whether that one is yours to use depends on the provider and the setup, so ask your water provider before you rely on it. In a real emergency, the main valve at the house is the one to use.

How to close and test the main valve

Practice this once when nothing is wrong. A valve that has not moved in years can be stiff, and you want to learn that before a flood.

  1. 1Locate and identify the valve. Check the handle. A wheel is a gate valve and a lever is a ball valve.
  2. 2Close it slowly by hand. Do not use pliers or a wrench on a gate valve. Stop when it is firm.
  3. 3Open a faucet to confirm. The flow should die to a trickle. If water keeps running, the valve is not sealing.
  4. 4Reopen it slowly. Open the valve fully, then back it off a quarter turn on a gate valve so it does not stick.
  5. 5Tag it. Hang a tag or mark the wall so anyone in the home can find it.

Whole house shutoff or a fixture stop

Not every leak needs the main closed. Picking the smaller shutoff saves the household water for everything else.

Use a fixture stop valve

  • A dripping faucet or supply line under a sink
  • A toilet that is overflowing or running
  • A leaking dishwasher or washing machine hose
  • A leaking hose bib with its own control

Use the main valve

  • A burst pipe or a gushing supply line
  • Water through the ceiling or running inside a wall
  • A failed water heater connection
  • A leak you cannot find or reach

After closing the main, drain the lines and call. If you suspect a slow leak that comes and goes, leak detection can find the source without opening finished walls.

If the valve will not turn or will not seal

A stuck valve is a common surprise. Scale builds around the stem in very hard water, and Goleta Water District's 2025 report gives a customer average of 21 grains per gallon. Do not force a seized valve with a pipe wrench, since a snapped stem can turn a small problem into a flood.

A valve that closes but does not stop the flow is worn out. Either way, a plumber can replace it with a ball valve, which is simpler to operate. Having a reliable main valve is worth the visit.
