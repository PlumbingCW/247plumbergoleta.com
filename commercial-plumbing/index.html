After hours: what to tell us and what to do first

A commercial building has more at stake than a house. These four steps keep the problem small until a plumber arrives.

Name the unit and the access

Give the building address, the suite number, who holds the keys and whether a lockbox or alarm code is needed. A work-order style call saves time at the door.

Isolate the water

Close the building main or the tenant shutoff. If you do not know where it is, the property manager or the last plumber's tag usually does.

Protect the floor and the stock

Move product, paper and electronics away from the water. In a restaurant, shut off the fryers and cooking equipment near a flooding drain.

Photograph and note

Take photos of the water level and the damage, and write down when you first noticed it. Your landlord and your insurer will ask.

Restaurants that lose their only restroom or kitchen drain usually have to close, so say so on the call. Same-day and after-hours dispatch is available for these jobs.

Grease traps and restaurant kitchens

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oils and grease before they enter the building drain. When it fills, grease passes through and hardens in the line, and the kitchen drain slows then stops. Goleta Sanitary District asks customers to keep fats, oils and grease out of the drain, so a full trap is also a sanitary-district problem, not just a plumbing one.

Trap size, pumping frequency and the haulers that may be used depend on the building and the district. Many operators schedule pumping when the trap is about one quarter full of solids, rather than waiting for a backup. Ask the sanitary district that serves your address for its grease rules, and keep the pumping records on site.

We clear the kitchen line behind the trap and check the vents. A grease trap that needs pumping is not a drain cleaning job alone, and the hauler may need to be a separate licensed service.

Backflow testing and cross-connection control

A backflow preventer stops dirty water from being pulled back into the clean supply. Goleta Water District says a device is often required because irrigation, pools, water softeners or leaks can create back pressure. Devices on approved projects must be tested annually by a state-certified tester, and the district's Cross Connection Specialist can tell you what your property needs.

A failed test is a plumbing job, not a paperwork one. Replacing a backflow preventer falls under a C-36 plumbing contractor licence per CSLB. If your annual test is due, or the device is leaking from the relief port, call and we plan the visit.

Storm drains, sump pumps and ejector pumps

Commercial lots collect rain through catch basins and drain lines, and leaves, trash and silt block them. Goleta gets about 17 inches of rain a year, mostly in winter, so cleaning a lot's storm drain before January is cheaper than clearing it during a downpour. We can flush and camera a private storm line.

If your building has a sump or an ejector pump, a failed pump shows up as water in a low floor drain or an alarm. A sump pump handles groundwater, while an ejector lifts waste from below-grade restrooms up to the sewer. This applies only if your building has one, and a failed unit is repaired or replaced.

Leak detection and honest limits

A hidden leak in a commercial building often shows up as a high water bill or a damp spot with no visible source. Leak detection uses listening gear and pressure checks to narrow the search before walls are opened. Not every leak can be found without some demolition.

We are licensed plumbers, but some building problems belong to other trades. Fire sprinkler systems, electrical work, HVAC and roof drains on a flat roof may need a separate licensed contractor. We will tell you at the visit rather than start work we should not.
