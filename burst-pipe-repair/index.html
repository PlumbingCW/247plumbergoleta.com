The first 60 seconds: stop the water

Do these in order. You do not need tools, and each step cuts the amount of water that reaches your floors.

  1. 1Find and close the main water valve. It is usually at the front of the house, near the water heater, or at the meter box. Turn a gate valve clockwise until it stops, or a lever handle a quarter turn across the pipe.
  2. 2Open a faucet to drain the line. Open a tap on the lowest floor, and one upstairs, so water in the pipes drains out instead of out the break.
  3. 3Turn off power near the water. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off those breakers only if you can reach them without standing in water. Otherwise stay back and call.
  4. 4Shut off the water heater. Cut the electricity to an electric unit or set a gas unit to pilot, so the tank does not heat while it drains.
  5. 5Save what you can. Move belongings off the floor, photograph the damage, and call the 24/7 line.

If you smell gas, leave the building first and call your gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.

What makes a pipe burst or crack

A pipe fails when the pressure inside exceeds what the weakest spot can hold. Common causes are corrosion that thins the wall, age, a loose or failed joint, a pressure spike after a valve closes fast, and accidental damage from a nail, a shovel or a tool. Ground movement can also strain buried and slab lines.

Goleta Water District reports a customer average of 21 grains per gallon, and hard water leaves scale that narrows pipes and raises friction. Scale does not cause bursts by itself, but a narrowed, aging line is more likely to fail at a joint. If one pipe has failed, ask the plumber to look at the neighbors in the same run.

Replacement pipe and fittings should meet the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so ask the plumber which materials are being used before the wall is closed.

When a broken pipe cannot wait: what to do now

Some pipe failures can be managed with the main valve off. These cannot, because the damage grows by the minute.

Water is pouring or spraying

A spray from a split pipe soaks walls and ceilings quickly. Close the main valve, open a faucet to drain, and call right away.

Water is in a ceiling or wall cavity

Trapped water bows drywall and can drop all at once. Shut off the main and keep people out from underneath.

The only bathroom or kitchen is down

A burst supply valve or pipe under the only sink or toilet leaves a household without water. Say so on the call.

A drain line is leaking sewage

A broken drain pipe puts wastewater into the crawlspace or slab. Stop using water and call the 24/7 line.

Cracked, leaking and pinhole pipes

Not every failure is a dramatic burst. A crack or pinhole leaks slowly, which is why it can go unnoticed. Each type of failure needs a slightly different repair.

Cracked or broken pipe

A crack from a hit, a bad joint or age lets water seep along the pipe. The plumber cuts back to sound pipe on both sides, installs a new section with proper fittings and tests the line under pressure. Patches and wraps are only stopgaps.

Copper pipe repair

Copper pipe develops pinholes where the wall has corroded. One pinhole can be cut out and the pipe re-joined. A run with several pinholes is a warning that the rest of the line is thin, and the plumber will say if a longer replacement is wiser.

Drain pipe repair

A broken drain pipe under a sink, in a wall or beneath the floor leaks wastewater and not clean water. The fix is a cut-out and replacement with matching pipe. See drain cleaning if the problem turns out to be a clog and not a break.

Supply valves and leaks at fixtures

A burst or broken shutoff valve under a sink or toilet is common and quick to fix. The plumber replaces the valve and its supply line. For the faucet itself, see faucet repair.

After the water is off: drying, checks and what comes next

Once the break is repaired and the line is tested, drying is the next priority. Water left in drywall, insulation and subfloors can grow mold within a couple of days. Fans, open windows and a dehumidifier help, and wet insulation usually has to come out.

A burst often exposes a wider problem. If the pipe that failed was old, ask about a whole-house repipe, and if the water was hiding for weeks before it broke, leak detection can check the rest of the system. A leak under a concrete floor is a different job, covered under slab leak repair.
