Del Playa Drive, the bluff and leaks that cannot sit

Del Playa Drive runs about a mile along the bluff and is among the oldest roads in Isla Vista. The Independent names bluff erosion as a stated local issue. A running leak near a bluff edge is a reason to shut off the water at once and say so when you call.

One street up, El Nido Lane is lined with apartments, which usually means several households on one drain. Farther inland, Sabado Tarde, Trigo and Pasado carry their own mix of rentals. Wherever you are, give the dispatcher the street, the unit and which fixtures are affected.

Before We Arrive: four steps for a shared Isla Vista house

Shared housing needs a few extra moves, because the water shutoff and the panel may belong to someone else.

Shut off the main water valve

If you cannot find the building shutoff, close the stop valve under the sink or behind the toilet and text your landlord for the main.

Cut power to wet areas

Switch off the circuit for a flooded room only if you can reach the panel on dry floor. Unplug power strips resting in water.

Clear the area

Move laptops, bikes, boxes and bedding out of the wet room, and keep people and pets away from sewage.

Call the 24/7 line

Wipes, grease and the sewer system behind Isla Vista

Goleta West Sanitary District collects sewage across Western Goleta Valley and Isla Vista, with about 6,100 connections, and the Goleta Sanitary District plant treats it. The Isla Vista Community Services District does not provide water or sewer service. Goleta Water District serves the Goleta Valley, and Isla Vista falls inside its service area according to local reporting.

Goleta Sanitary District's newsletter asks customers to flush only human waste and toilet paper, and to keep fats, oils and grease out of the drain. Wipes do not break down the way paper does. If the whole house backs up, the cause may be a stoppage past your cleanout, so sewer line cleaning or a district call may be the next step.
