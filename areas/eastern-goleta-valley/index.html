Check your water bill first: which provider serves your street

Eastern Goleta Valley is an unincorporated area between the City of Goleta and Santa Barbara. Depending on the street, water comes from Goleta Water District or another provider, so check your bill. The answer matters because the district, not the plumber, owns the meter and the main.

Goleta Water District draws mostly on Lake Cachuma, treated at the Corona del Mar plant, plus groundwater. Its 2025 water quality report gives 360 mg/L hardness for Cachuma water and 470 mg/L for groundwater, and a customer average of 21 grains per gallon. The district itself calls the water very hard.

What 21 grains per gallon does inside a water heater

At 21 grains per gallon, a gas or electric tank heater collects scale on its elements and sediment at the bottom. The layer insulates the burner or element from the water, so the heater works harder and runs out sooner. Rumbling or popping sounds from the tank are the usual sign.

A water heater repair visit can flush the tank, check the anode rod and test the relief valve. If the tank itself is leaking, repair is not possible and the conversation turns to replacement.

Your lateral, your meter and the district: who handles what

Sewer and water responsibilities split at the property line. This is a simplified picture, and the district has the final say.

Usually the owner's job

  • The sewer lateral from the building to the street main, according to Goleta Sanitary District's FAQ
  • Fixtures, supply lines and drains inside the house
  • A water heater, its venting and its relief line
  • Hose bibs and irrigation on your side of the meter

Ask the district

  • The street main and anything past the cleanout on the public side
  • Which sanitary district collects at your address, Goleta Sanitary or Goleta West
  • Rules about work in a district easement, which needs a permit
  • Whether a leak adjustment is possible on a high water bill

Both Goleta Sanitary District and Goleta West Sanitary District may overlap this area, so ask the district for your street.

Before We Arrive: four steps for Eastern Goleta Valley homes

Most homes here are owner-occupied, so you control the main valve and the panel. Use them.

Shut off the main water valve

Find it near the meter at the street or where the line enters the house. Turn it fully clockwise. If you share a meter, ask the neighbor.

Cut power to wet areas

Switch off the breaker for a flooded garage or laundry only if you can stand on dry ground to reach the panel.

Clear the area

Move vehicles, boxes and rugs away from water. Keep children and pets out of sewage, and photograph the damage.

Is the water bill hiding a leak? A five-minute meter check

Do this before calling about a bill you cannot explain.

  1. 1Turn off every fixture. Close taps, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one flushes.
  2. 2Find the meter. It sits in a box near the street. Lift the lid, and read the dial.
  3. 3Look at the leak indicator. A small triangle or wheel spins when any water moves through.
  4. 4Wait fifteen minutes and read again. Any change means water is escaping somewhere past the meter.
  5. 5Call us with what you saw. Describe the dial and where the house feels damp or warm.
