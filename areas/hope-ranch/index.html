Signs a Hope Ranch plumbing problem needs a call today

Large hillside properties hide trouble for a while. These signs mean it is time to phone.

Soggy ground in the yard

A wet patch that does not dry out may be a broken service line or a drain field problem.

Drains gurgle in several rooms

If the property is on septic, that points to a full tank or a blocked outlet.

Low pressure in the upper bathroom

Hilly terrain makes pressure uneven, and a pressure reducing valve can fail.

The meter runs with nothing on

Long runs of pipe leak out of sight for weeks.

A tank heater drips at the base

The tank is corroded and a replacement is the likely answer.

Sewer smell near a cleanout

Waste is standing in the line between house and main or tank.

Septic or sewer? Start by finding out

If your property is not on a public sewer, county Chapter 18C governs its septic system. A county permit is required to construct, repair, modify, abandon or destroy a septic system.

Routine maintenance by a qualified contractor needs no permit if a written report is filed within 30 days, and the owner is responsible for inspections and fixing violations. So when drains gurgle in several rooms, the plumber checks whether the problem is inside the house or in the tank, and tells you what needs a county permit.

If the line runs to a public sewer, a sewer camera inspection shows roots, breaks or a bellied section. Oak woodland on the hillsides is a reason roots are worth checking.

Repairs on the estate lots here are made to the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and permit questions go to the county building office.

Before We Arrive: four steps for a Hope Ranch property

Shut off the main water valve

Water from La Cumbre Mutual Water Company

La Cumbre Mutual Water Company is a private mutual water company serving Hope Ranch and adjacent annex areas, about 2,100 acres with more than 1,400 service connections. Its sources include State Water Project water delivered through Lake Cachuma and treated by the City of Santa Barbara, purchased water and shared groundwater with Goleta Water District.

We do not state its hardness or disinfectant, because we have not verified them. Ask La Cumbre for its water quality report if you are choosing a softener or a filter, or if you want to know what is going into your tank heater.

Permits, the association and who approves the work

Hope Ranch is unincorporated, so County Building and Safety enforces plumbing code and issues permits. The Hope Ranch Park Homes Association also has an Architectural Board of Review for exterior changes. A new tank or outdoor equipment that changes what is seen from outside may need its approval.

Permit rules vary, so ask County Building and Safety first, and ask the association about anything exterior. We are licensed and insured plumbers and we explain the steps before work starts.
