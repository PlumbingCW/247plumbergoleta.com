Storm season in Montecito: what January 2018 taught homeowners

On 9 January 2018, about half an inch of rain fell in five minutes around 3:30 a.m., and the debris flow killed 23 people, according to the USGS. The county counted 129 single-family homes destroyed and 307 damaged. Montecito Water District reported damage at over 300 locations, including 15 distribution main breaks and about 290 damaged service connections.

For a homeowner the lesson is practical. After a heavy storm, check for low pressure, discolored water and wet ground near the service line, and shut the main if something looks wrong. A leak detection visit can separate a damaged line from a fixture problem.

Montecito Water District hardness and your water heater

Montecito Water District serves Montecito and Summerland, about 4,600 connections. Its 2023 consumer confidence report lists average hardness of 383 mg/L for Cachuma water, 400 for Jameson Lake and 411 for groundwater, roughly 23 to 24 grains per gallon for surface water, with free chlorine averaging 0.79 mg/L.

That is hard water, and it leaves scale on heater elements and tank bottoms. If your heater has failed, water heater replacement is the next conversation, and filtration or a softener is a choice you can add.

Montecito Sanitary District and the septic question

Montecito Sanitary District serves 3,043 residential and 44 commercial or institutional connections, and offers a Septic to Sewer conversion program with an installment plan. How many properties remain on septic is not confirmed. If yours is one of them, the district is the place to ask about connecting.

Which side of the property line a stoppage sits on decides who fixes it. A camera shows where the blockage is. The lateral rule was not verified for this district, so ask the district.

Upper Village, East Valley Road and estate-size properties

Montecito is almost entirely single-family estates, and 75.8 percent of homes are owner-occupied. Journal reports describe the Golden Quadrangle as multi-acre lots north of East Valley Road. Other places include Upper Village and Lower Village, Hedgerows, Fernald Point, Eucalyptus Hill, Romero Canyon, San Ysidro Road and Hot Springs Road.

On large lots, pipe runs are long and irrigation is extensive, so a small leak can lose a lot of water. A repiping decision depends on how often the same lines fail. Landmarks such as Lotusland and San Ysidro Ranch help the dispatcher locate you.
