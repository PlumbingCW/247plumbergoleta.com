Where Ellwood is

Ellwood is part of Goleta, the city where our shop sits on Hollister Avenue. A place-name directory lists Ellwood as a populated place inside the City of Goleta, and Ellwood School is within city limits. The city does not publish a boundary for the Ellwood neighborhood in the sources we checked, so use your street name when you call.

The best-known landmark is Ellwood Mesa Open Space, a 137-acre property the city took ownership of on February 4, 2005 after a land exchange with the owners and Comstock Homes. The city says its eucalyptus groves host overwintering monarch butterflies from October.

Santa Barbara Shores Park, also a city park, sits next to it. In July 2004 the Goleta City Council approved residential development by Comstock Homes on 36 acres of that park land as part of the exchange.

Condominiums, shared lines and who fixes what

Real estate listing sites, which are not official sources, show a mix of condominium communities and houses near Ellwood Beach Drive, Santa Barbara Shores Drive and along Hollister Avenue. Your own deed tells you which kind of home you have.

In a condo, some pipes belong to you and some belong to the association. A common rule is that lines serving only your unit are yours and shared stacks and mains are the association's, but rules differ, so check your governing documents.

Call your manager before a plumber when a leak may come from a neighbor's unit or a shared line. Then call us for anything inside your own walls. For a house nearby, Old Town Goleta has its own notes. Giving the unit number and a contact for access speeds the visit.

Hard water, and a note on older Ellwood housing

Goleta Water District's 2025 water quality report gives a customer average hardness of 21 grains per gallon, and the district calls the water very hard. Scale builds on heating elements and in tanks, so a yearly flush is worth asking about. A softener or filter is a choice, not a repair.

The city's historic inventory notes that oil leaseholders built field workers' housing near the highway after the 1928 Ellwood oil strike. That is history, and it does not mean any current home has old plumbing. Check the year built on county records before assuming anything.
