Cold bursts, lukewarm water and what to do about error codes

Tankless units fail in recognizable ways. These are the symptoms callers describe most, and what each usually points to.

A cold burst between two hot ones

Shut the tap briefly and reopen it, and a slug of cold water arrives before the heat returns. This cold water sandwich happens because pipe water cools while the unit is off. A recirculation setup or a small buffer tank can shorten it.

Lukewarm output

Scale on the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet filter restricts flow, so the unit cannot reach the set temperature. A flush often restores it.

No hot water at low flow

Most tankless units need a minimum flow, often around half a gallon per minute, to fire. A barely open tap may not trigger the burner.

Short cycling

A unit that fires, shuts down and fires again may have a flame sensor fault, a gas supply that is too small or a venting restriction.

Many units show a code on a display. Look it up in the manual or give it to the dispatcher with the model number.

The tankless flush: how descaling works

A flush dissolves scale from the heat exchanger. The manufacturer's instructions set the solution and time, and these are the general steps.

  1. 1Shut off power and fuel. The unit is turned off, gas is closed and the water is isolated at the service valves.
  2. 2Connect a pump and hoses. A small pump circulates a descaling solution, often food-grade white vinegar or a made-for-purpose cleaner, through the heater.
  3. 3Circulate for the set time. The solution runs through the heat exchanger for the time the manufacturer specifies, often around an hour.
  4. 4Rinse and clean the filter. The unit is flushed with fresh water and the inlet screen is pulled and cleaned.
  5. 5Restore and test. Valves are reopened, air is purged, and the plumber checks flow, temperature and codes.

Service valves, also called isolation valves, make this flush possible. A unit installed without them can still be flushed, but the plumber may need to add them.

Why 21 grains per gallon makes flushing matter

The Goleta Water District 2025 report gives a customer average of 21 grains per gallon. A tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages, and a thin layer of scale there reduces flow and heat transfer. The unit then burns more gas to deliver less, overheats and may shut down on a high-limit code.

That is why makers often recommend a flush at least once a year in hard-water areas. A softener or filter ahead of the unit can lengthen the interval, as explained on the softener page.

Installing tankless: gas line, venting and condensate

A tankless gas unit can need a gas line larger than the one that fed a tank, often 3/4 inch, because the burner draws a lot of gas at once. The plumber sizes the line to the unit's input and the length of the run. Gas piping and appliance work fall under a licensed plumbing contractor.

Venting changes too. Condensing tankless units are efficient enough that their exhaust is cool, so they use plastic vent pipe, and they produce acidic condensate that must drain through a neutralizer to a proper drain. Non-condensing units use stainless steel vent. If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.

Service it or replace it?

Many tankless problems are service problems. Here is how the two paths compare.

Service the unit

  • Scale, a dirty inlet filter or a clogged condensate line
  • A single failed sensor, igniter or flow part
  • A unit that still heats at full flow after cleaning

Replace the unit

  • A cracked or leaking heat exchanger
  • Repeated high-limit trips after flushing
  • Parts no longer available for an old model

We service as well as replace, and the plumber tells you which applies after testing.
