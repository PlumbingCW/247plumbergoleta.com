A tank that leaks from the shell cannot be fixed

A water heater tank is a steel vessel with a glass lining. When the lining cracks and the steel corrodes through, water escapes from the base or the seam and the failure is permanent. Patches, sealants and tightening do not hold against 40 to 60 pounds of line pressure.

Most tanks last roughly eight to twelve years, and very hard water can shorten that. The plumber confirms where the water comes from before calling it, because a leaking fitting or valve is a cheaper repair, covered on the water heater repair page.

Water on the floor: do this before the plumber arrives

A flooding heater can empty its tank in minutes and keep refilling from the supply line. These steps limit the damage.

Shut the cold supply valve

Turn the handle on the pipe above the heater a quarter turn if it is a ball valve, or clockwise if it is a gate valve. If the valve is stuck, close the main water shutoff at the house.

Cut power or gas

Flip the heater's breaker off. On a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to off. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.

Smell gas? Leave first

If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.

Move belongings and photograph the damage

Lift boxes, cardboard and electronics off the floor. Take photos of the water line and the heater label for the insurance claim.

Do not drain the tank into the garage floor. The plumber connects a hose to the drain valve and runs it outside or to a drain.

Why Goleta tanks wear out sooner

The Goleta Water District 2025 water quality report gives a customer average of 21 grains per gallon, and the district's own page calls the supply very hard. Dissolved calcium and magnesium bake onto the tank floor and heating elements as scale.

A magnesium or aluminum anode rod sacrifices itself to protect the steel. In hard water the rod is consumed faster, and once it is gone the tank wall takes the corrosion. A heater that has never had its rod checked often fails before the label date. A softener or filter reduces scale on the next heater, so it is worth asking about when you replace.

Sizing the new heater by household

A tank is rated by gallons, but the number that matters is first-hour rating, the hot water it delivers in the first hour of heavy use. A one- or two-person home often does well with a 30 to 40 gallon tank. A family of four usually looks at 50 gallons, and a larger household with several bathrooms may need more or a high-recovery model.

Rentals near UC Santa Barbara and in Isla Vista can have many people sharing one heater, which pushes the size up. The plumber asks how many people and bathrooms are on the heater and sizes from there, instead of copying the old tank.

Swapping gas for a heat pump water heater

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank, which uses far less electricity than a standard electric element. Switching from gas to a heat pump unit changes three things in the house.

Electrical

Most heat pump tanks need a dedicated 240-volt circuit, often 30 amps, so the panel must have room. A gas house may not have a 240-volt line near the heater, and the electrician or plumber must run one.

Space and air

The unit cools the air around it, and manufacturers specify how much open volume or ducting it needs. A tight closet that suited a gas tank can starve a heat pump, and a garage is often a better home.

Condensate

Pulling heat from air also pulls moisture, which collects as condensate and needs a drain or pump. The line has to run to a floor drain or a pump, and the plumber plans that run before you buy.

What the emergency replacement visit covers

A good replacement is more than lifting one tank out and putting another in.

  1. 1Shut off and drain. Water, power or gas is isolated and the old tank is drained to a hose.
  2. 2Remove the old unit. Connectors are disconnected, the tank is carried out and the area is checked for rot or damage.
  3. 3Set and strap. The new tank sits on a level base, in a drain pan where required, and is strapped to the wall.
  4. 4Connect and test. Water, relief valve discharge, gas or electric and venting are connected, then leak-tested before power or gas comes on.

A planned swap follows the same steps with more choice about type and placement, covered on the installation page.

Permits and strapping in Goleta and nearby

The City of Santa Barbara issues a same-location water heater replacement as an OnDemand permit with no plan review for one- and two-family homes, and a contractor licence is required to obtain it. For Goleta, permit rules vary, so ask City of Goleta Building and Safety. In unincorporated areas, ask County Building and Safety.

California Health and Safety Code 19211 requires replacement water heaters up to 120 gallons that have an off-the-shelf kit to be braced, anchored or strapped. Straps are part of every swap we plan. For a flood beyond the heater, see burst pipe repair. Residents of Isla Vista and Santa Barbara ask about permit offices often.
