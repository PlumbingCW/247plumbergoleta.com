Sewer Camera Inspection in Goleta, CA

A sewer camera inspection in Goleta, CA sends a small waterproof camera through your sewer line to show what is inside. It replaces guessing with a picture of the pipe, and it is the best way to understand repeat clogs or check a line before a purchase.

What the camera shows you

A sewer camera head sits on a flexible push rod and carries a bright light. It travels from a cleanout, a pulled toilet or a roof vent toward the street main, sending live video to a screen. The plumber watches the whole run and pauses on anything unusual.

A typical lateral is 4 inches in diameter, so the camera head is small enough to pass bends and joints. Clean pipe looks smooth and consistent. Defects stand out as breaks in that pattern, and their position is measured by a counter on the push rod.

What a camera shows is judged against the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), for slope, joints and cleanout access, so ask for the recording as part of the visit.

Locating the line and marking it

Many owners do not know where their sewer line runs. A small transmitter called a sonde can ride just behind the camera head. A handheld receiver above ground picks up its signal and shows both the position and the depth of the pipe.

That is how a plumber can mark the line on the lawn or driveway with paint or flags. The marks help you plan a fence, a tree or an addition without crossing the line, and they let a repair crew dig in the right place. If a leak is suspected, ask about leak detection as well.

When a sewer inspection is worth booking

You do not need to wait for a backup. These moments are good times to look.

  • ✓Before buying a home. A seller's inspection report rarely covers the lateral. A camera look can reveal roots or a belly before closing.
  • ✓After a repeat clog. If a line clogs again within weeks of a cleaning, a camera finds the cause.
  • ✓Before lining or a major repair. Lining only works in sound pipe, so inspect it first.
  • ✓After tree work or a remodel. Roots, heavy equipment and new loads can disturb a lateral.
  • ✓When an older home shows slow drains throughout. Age alone does not mean failure, but a camera tells you if the pipe is aging well.

What the camera cannot do

A camera shows the inside surface of the pipe. It cannot see through soil, so it cannot tell you how much ground has washed out around a crack. A long line with many bends or standing water may limit how far the camera travels, and a full line must be cleared before it can be inspected.

It also cannot prove a pipe is watertight. A hairline crack may not be visible, and a very old pipe can look fine on video yet fail under load. The plumber will say what was seen and what was not. If a line is too clogged to inspect, sewer line cleaning may come first.

The result you take home

Ask for a recording and a short written summary. A good record lists the date, the entry point, where each defect sits in feet from that point, and a plain description of what it is. Photos or video clips of the worst spots are helpful too.

You can show that record to your district, an insurer or another contractor. If you are in a dispute over who owns a problem, a recording shows exactly where it sits.
