What a proper installation includes

Installing a water heater involves more than bolting a tank into a corner. It covers the water connections, the gas or electric supply, a vent or exhaust path, a temperature and pressure relief valve, and a safe place for that valve to drain.

The California Plumbing Code sets minimum requirements for these parts, and a plumber follows it so the heater is safe on the first day and the tenth year. Parts a buyer rarely sees on a quote, such as the discharge pipe, are the ones inspectors and insurers notice. If the old unit failed unexpectedly, the same work is done faster on the water heater replacement page.

A clean install, step by step

The order of work matters. This is what a careful install looks like.

  1. 1Walk the site. The plumber measures the space, checks the gas line size, the electric panel and the route for venting and drainage.
  2. 2Isolate and remove. Water, power and gas are shut off. The old heater is drained and taken out.
  3. 3Set the new unit. The tank goes on a level base with a drain pan where required and is strapped to the wall studs.
  4. 4Connect water and fuel. New shutoff valves and flexible connectors go on, then the gas line or the 240-volt circuit.
  5. 5Fit the safety devices. The relief valve and its discharge pipe go in, with an expansion tank if the system has a check valve.
  6. 6Test and show you. Leaks, gas joints, venting and temperature are tested. You get shown the shutoffs.

The code-driven parts to expect on a quote

These items are easy to leave off a low estimate, then show up as extras. Ask whether each one is included.

Expansion tank

When a water meter has a check valve or a pressure reducing valve, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small expansion tank, often 2 gallons, absorbs it and protects the tank and relief valve.

Shutoff valve

A full-port ball valve on the cold line lets you stop the water in an emergency. Old gate valves often seize.

Relief valve and discharge pipe

The relief valve opens before pressure or heat gets dangerous, and a solid pipe carries the discharge to a safe spot. It should never be capped.

Drain pan and strapping

A pan under the tank catches slow leaks, and Health and Safety Code 19211 requires strapping on new and replacement heaters up to 120 gallons with an off-the-shelf kit.

Gas heaters add venting and a gas shutoff. If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.

Where the heater goes: garage, closet or outside

The location sets the rules. A garage needs the burner raised or protected on gas units, and the heater needs a pan. A closet needs combustion air and a vent path. An outside wall suits tankless units, though the exhaust still needs clearance from windows.

In Old Town Goleta along Hollister Avenue and in apartments near UC Santa Barbara, space is tight and a heater often sits in a closet. Measure the closet door too, since a new tank must fit through it. A tankless unit or a heat pump may suit the layout better than another standard tank.

What to ask any plumber before you sign

These questions help you compare quotes fairly.

  • ✓Is the plumber licensed for this work? Look up the licence on the Contractors State License Board lookup. A number in an ad does not prove it is valid.
  • ✓Is there a written contract? A written contract is required over $500 and must name the licence and who pulls permits.
  • ✓How much is the down payment? State rules limit it to the lesser of $1,000 or 10 percent of the contract price, in most cases.
  • ✓Who pulls the permit? Permit rules vary, so ask City of Goleta Building and Safety, or County Building and Safety for unincorporated areas.
  • ✓Which code items are included? Expansion tank, pan, relief piping, straps and venting should be listed.

Sizing, hard water and what to do around the heater

Size by first-hour rating and household, not by what the last tank was. Two to three people often suit 40 gallons, and four or more usually want 50. The Goleta Water District report gives 21 grains per gallon, so consider a softener or filter to slow scale on the new unit.

If the install also changes pipe routes, the project may overlap with general plumbing installation or repiping work. Customers in Eastern Goleta Valley and Hope Ranch should confirm their permit authority with the county first.
